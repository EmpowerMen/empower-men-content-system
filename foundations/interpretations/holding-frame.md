# Holding Frame

---

## Introduction

Owen Cook’s discussion on “frame,” social dynamics, and male-female interaction shares deep conceptual overlap with Dr. Robert Moore’s archetypal model of masculine psychology: the King, Warrior, Magician, and Lover (KWML). This translation reframes Cook’s ideas into Moore’s language, clarifying the psychological and energetic dynamics at play.

---

## 1. Frame as the King Archetype

- **Owen Cook:** “Frame” is the paradigm or lens through which reality is perceived and controlled. Men “holding frame” means setting the terms of interaction, controlling the narrative, and establishing authority.

- **KWML Translation:** This corresponds to the **King** archetype — the mature, centered ruler who holds sovereignty and order. The King embodies calm authority and sets the tone for relationships and social environments. When a man “holds frame,” he is embodying the King’s role of creating structure and blessing.

- **Insight:** A man who holds frame is not reactive or desperate but sovereign and grounded, providing safety and direction.

---

## 2. Women’s Frame Control as the Lover and Warrior Archetypes

- **Owen Cook:** Women are masters of controlling the frame, positioning themselves as the “prize.” They selectively use attention and have a biological “ick” mechanism that protects them from harmful connections.

- **KWML Translation:** The **Lover** archetype represents emotional openness, connection, and vitality — it is boundaryless and receptive. However, the **Warrior** archetype is responsible for setting and enforcing boundaries, vigilance, and protection against danger.

- The woman’s “ick” response is a manifestation of the **Warrior’s** protective function, signaling perceived threat or danger and enforcing boundaries to ensure survival and emotional safety.

- **Insight:** Women’s selective attention and “ick” response arise from the Lover’s emotional sensitivity combined with the Warrior’s boundary enforcement and vigilance.

---

## 3. Men’s Pressure and Overthinking as Warrior Shadow and Lover Underdevelopment

- **Owen Cook:** Men feel pressure to be “on their A game” to avoid triggering the “ick,” leading to nervousness, overthinking, and transactional behavior.

- **KWML Translation:** This state often reflects an **overactive Warrior shadow** (rigid, controlling, anxious) combined with an **underdeveloped Lover** (lack of emotional openness and connection). The Warrior, when healthy, is disciplined and focused; when shadowed, it becomes anxious and reactive. The Lover, when underdeveloped, leaves men disconnected from their feelings and vulnerable to overthinking.

- **Insight:** Nervousness and overthinking indicate imbalance. The man is not fully embodying the calm, confident King, nor the open, connected Lover.

---

## 4. Flipping the Buyer-Seller Dynamic as Magician Strategy and King Authority

- **Owen Cook:** Luxury brands and exclusivity flip the buyer-seller dynamic through scarcity and social proof, creating high value and control.

- **KWML Translation:** This is the domain of the **Magician** archetype, who understands hidden patterns, strategy, and transformation. The Magician manipulates social dynamics and symbols (scarcity, exclusivity) to create perceived value. The **King’s authority** is maintained through ritual and social order, reinforcing status and hierarchy.

- **Insight:** The Magician’s insight combined with the King’s sovereignty shapes social reality and value systems.

---

## 5. The “Ick” Mechanism as Warrior’s Protective Boundary

- **Owen Cook:** The “ick” is a biological survival response that causes retreat and rejection.

- **KWML Translation:** The **Warrior’s shadow** includes vigilance, boundary enforcement, and rejection mechanisms designed to protect from harm. This “ick” is a manifestation of the Warrior’s role in safeguarding physical and emotional well-being.

- **Insight:** Understanding and respecting the Warrior’s protective boundaries is essential for authentic connection.

---

## 6. Integrating King, Warrior, and Lover to “Shut Off the Ick”

- **Owen Cook:** The solution is to “shut off the ick mechanism” by engaging a different part of the brain and switching the frame.

- **KWML Translation:** This requires integration of the **King’s calm authority**, the **Warrior’s disciplined boundaries**, and the **Lover’s genuine connection**. A man who embodies the King’s sovereignty, the Warrior’s protective vigilance, and the Lover’s emotional openness creates safety and attraction, allowing the “ick” to relax.

- **Insight:** Mature masculinity balances authority with boundaries and vulnerability, power with empathy.

---

## 7. Summary: Healthy Masculinity as KWML Integration

Owen Cook’s talk highlights the challenges men face in social and romantic dynamics, which can be understood as archetypal imbalances:

| Problematic State | Archetypal Imbalance | Healthy Integration |
|-------------------|---------------------|---------------------|
| Over-controlling, anxious, “on edge” men | Warrior shadow dominant, Lover underdeveloped, King under-expressed | Balanced King (sovereignty), Warrior (disciplined boundaries), Lover (emotional openness), Magician (insight) |
| Women’s “ick” rejection | Warrior’s protective boundary activated | Safe, authentic connection through King-Warrior-Lover integration |
| Transactional, nervous interactions | Lack of mature King, Warrior, and Lover presence | Confident, grounded, emotionally attuned masculinity |

---

## Conclusion

Reframing Owen Cook’s concepts through Robert Moore’s KWML archetypes deepens understanding of masculine psychology:

- **King** = Holds frame, authority, and order  
- **Warrior** = Protects boundaries, enforces vigilance, and acts with disciplined focus  
- **Magician** = Strategizes social dynamics and symbolic meaning  
- **Lover** = Connects emotionally, open and boundaryless  

Men who integrate these archetypes maturely can hold frame authentically, engage women without triggering “ick,” and navigate social dynamics with confidence and empathy.
